# Changelog

Versions follow [semantic versioning](https://semver.org). All three packages share one version.

## 1.1.0 — 2026-10-31

- `shipbill.js`: on a website that isn't in **Payments → Website integration → Allowed websites**, `onError` (a buy button's `shipbill:error`) gets `origin_not_allowed` at once.
  - `ShipBill.checkout.open(clientToken)` then opens the checkout page itself straight away, so the sale still happens. It used to show a spinner for 15 seconds first.
  - A buy button's buyer reads "This checkout can't open on this website. If you're buying, contact the seller." until they close it, and closing it doesn't call `onClose`. The overlay used to flash and go.
- `shipbill.js`: `ShipBill.checkout.open(clientToken)` asks ShipBill whether this website may show the checkout before framing it (`GET /v1/embed-frames/{client_token}`, which needs no key). When ShipBill doesn't answer, the checkout is framed as before.
- `shipbill.js`: the console's errors begin with their code, as in `origin_not_allowed: …`.
- `shipbill.js`: buy buttons and `ShipBill.checkout.open` pass on the site before your page (its `document.referrer`, origin and path only), so **Analytics → Sources → Referred from** names where the buyer came from rather than your own site. A buyer who came straight to your page shows as "Direct or unknown". The frame's address gains `referrer`, and a buy button's request a `referrer` field.
- `@shipbill/node`: `paymentLinks.create` returns `warnings`; one with `code: "checkout_unavailable"` means no payment provider enabled in the link's mode can take it yet. A link's `available` is now `false` in that case too.
- `@shipbill/node`: `checkout.sessions.create` is refused with `checkout_unavailable` when none of the product's payment providers is enabled, where the session used to be created and couldn't be paid.
- `@shipbill/node`: a validation error's `message` names its field, as in `providers: Not connected: stripe.`, with `param` naming the field as before.
- `@shipbill/node`: `checkout.sessions.create` takes `attribution`: `page_url`, `referrer`, `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, and `utm_campaign`, where the buyer came from as your site remembers it. Analytics uses it before what the browser says, and keeps the addresses' origin and path only.
- `@shipbill/node`: a one-time product's `addons` (other one-time products of yours in its currency, each with a `quantity`, at most 19) and `buyer_quantity` (`min` and `max`, from 1 to 99: the quantity a buyer may choose on its payment link's and buy button's checkout), on `products.create`, `products.update`, and the product object.
- `@shipbill/node`: `paymentLinks.create` takes `items` (which of the product's add-ons the link includes, at what quantities) and `quantity` (a fixed quantity of the product). Both are set when the link is made and never changed. The link object carries them, and its `amount` is the whole set's.
- `@shipbill/node`: `paymentLinks.create` may return a warning with `code: "items_unavailable"`: the link's products share no payment provider enabled in its mode. `checkout.sessions.create` is refused with `items_unavailable` (on `line_items`) in that case, and when a set needs a copy at Polar or Creem and each already keeps 50.
- `@shipbill/node`: `subscriptions.endDiscount(id)` ends a subscriber's discount from their next renewal, and returns the subscription. It's refused with `no_discount` when there's none to end, and with `discount_not_endable` at Creem or Whop, which can't end one.
- `@shipbill/node`: a subscription's `discount` gains `ended_at` and `ended_reason` (`coupon_archived`, `coupon_used_up`, `coupon_ended`, or `ended_by_merchant`). A coupon that's archived, used up, or past its end date now ends the discount of every running subscription carrying it from its next renewal, with `subscription.updated`.
- `shipbill.js`: `data-shipbill-addons="prod_x,prod_y:2"` on a buy button is your page's own choice of the product's add-ons, each ID with its quantity after a colon (1 without one); the checkout then offers none. `ShipBill.checkout.open({ product, addons })` takes the same as `[{ product, quantity }]`. Without it, the checkout offers the product's add-ons marked Offer at the checkout, as boxes the buyer may tick.
- `@shipbill/node`: a subscription can carry a quantity and add-ons. Its object gains `items`: what renews each period, the product first, then any recurring add-ons, each with `quantity`, `unit_amount`, `amount`, `starts_at`, and `ends_at`. `amount` is their total. One-time add-ons are charged with the first payment only.
- `@shipbill/node`: `checkout.sessions.create` takes a subscription as the first of `line_items`, with only its own add-ons after it, and any `quantity`. It's refused with `items_unavailable` when no enabled provider can take a subscription with those add-ons (Polar and Creem take none; Whop no recurring ones), naming the providers.
- `@shipbill/node`: `subscriptions.setAddon(id, product, { renews })` drops a recurring add-on at the end of the period (`renews: false`) or adds one from the next renewal (`renews: true`), with nothing charged or refunded at the change; either undoes the other while it's still ahead. It's refused with `addon_not_changeable` when the subscription isn't running or is set to end, or the product isn't one of its recurring add-ons.
- `@shipbill/node`: a product's `addons` may be subscription products renewing as it does, on a subscription (recurring add-ons), and each has `offered`: offered at the checkout as a box the buyer may tick. `checkout.sessions.create` takes `offer_addons: true` to offer them. A payment link's `items` each have `offered`: Included in every sale, or Offered as a box.
- `@shipbill/node`: `paymentLinks.delete(id)` deletes a payment link for good (`DELETE /v1/payment-links/{id}`), answering `{ id, object: "payment_link", deleted: true }`. It leaves `paymentLinks.list`, and its address stops working as a disabled link's does, never reused; a checkout already open from it can finish. A second delete, or an update of a deleted link, answers 404 (`not_found`). To pause a link instead, update its `status` to `disabled`.

## 1.0.2 — 2026-10-22

- `shipbill.js`: with Settings → Checkout → Look on **Follow the buyer's device**, the checkout window now turns dark on a dark device in Firefox, in Safari, and in every browser on iPhone. These browsers tell a framed page the device's preference through the frame's color scheme, so 1.0.1's `light` kept the checkout light there, and 1.0.0's `normal` did in Safari. The frame is now `color-scheme: light dark`, like the checkout inside it, and stays see-through over a page whose own color scheme is dark.
- `shipbill.js`: the frame's address says `embed=2` instead of `embed=1`, so the checkout knows its frame is `light dark`. Checkouts opened by 1.0.1 and 1.0.0 (`embed=1`) look as they did.
- README: says the checkout's look is your profile's Look. A `theme` you pass applies only when **Let my website choose the embedded checkout's look** is on in Settings → Checkout; otherwise it is ignored, and the browser's console says so.

## 1.0.1 — 2026-10-21

- `shipbill.js`: the checkout window stays see-through over a page whose own color scheme is dark (`<meta name="color-scheme" content="dark">`, or `light dark` on a device set to dark). Such a page was covered by the checkout's background; the frame is now `color-scheme: light`, like the checkout inside it.
- `shipbill.js`: the checkout frame is named with `aria-label="Checkout"` instead of `title`, so desktop browsers no longer show a "Checkout" tooltip over the checkout. A test that finds the frame by `iframe[title=Checkout]` should use `iframe[aria-label=Checkout]`.

## 1.0.0 — 2026-10-21

The first release.

- `shipbill.js`: the checkout over the page (`ShipBill.checkout.open`, with a `client_token` or a product), buy buttons (`data-shipbill-product`), and price previews (`ShipBill.prices.get`). Embedded checkout protocol version 1.
- `@shipbill/js`: `loadShipBill` and the React hook `useShipBill`, typed.
- `shipbill.js`: the checkout window's look, `theme` (`"light"`, `"dark"`, `"system"`) in `ShipBill.checkout.open`'s options or a buy button's `data-shipbill-theme`, in place of the profile's checkout theme (the frame's `theme`, `x-shipbill-embed`).
- `shipbill.js`: `ShipBill.mount(next_action, element)` for a custom checkout: the provider's page, or Stripe's fields with `confirm()` and `destroy()`, from `mount-stripe.js` (loaded from the release's own folder on that call only, integrity-pinned), which loads Stripe.js from `js.stripe.com`; where ShipBill runs Stripe's stand-in, its fields page instead (`stand_in_url`). Typed in `@shipbill/js` (`NextAction`, `MountedAction`).
- `@shipbill/node`: every `/v1` operation, typed from ShipBill's specification (API version 2026-10-01), refunds, payment risk (`risk_status`, `risk_notes`, `payment.risk_cleared`), customers' checked tax numbers (`tax_number_country`, `tax_number_status`), invoice revisions (`revision`), credit notes for refunds (`creditNotes.list`, `creditNotes.retrieve`), invoice exports for accountants (`invoices.export`, a ZIP or CSV; it answers `invoice_export_pending` until the file is made), payment intents for a custom checkout (`paymentIntents.create`, `confirm`, `retrieve`, `cancel`; each of `payment_methods` with its `label`; a fields `next_action` with its `return_url`; a payment's `payment_intent`), a coupon's uses paged (`coupons.listRedemptions(id, { limit, starting_after, created_after })`), and payout events (`payout.created`, `payout.completed`, `payout.failed`, with a `Payout`) included; idempotent retries; lists that page themselves; `webhooks.verify` and `webhooks.sign`.
- `@shipbill/node`: `customers.erase` (`DELETE /v1/customers/{id}`) and a customer's `erased`, from ShipBill's current specification.
- `@shipbill/node`'s `User-Agent` names the runtime: `shipbill-node/1.0.0 node/22.11.0`.
